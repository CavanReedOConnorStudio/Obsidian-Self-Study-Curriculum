
## [[Minor VI ]]— [[Biology & Anatomy]]

[[Anatomy]], [[perception]], and [[botany]] are the sciences painters have always needed most directly. [[Renaissance]] [[figure painting]] depended on [[dissection]]; the study of [[vision]] and [[perception]] explains why certain painted [[illusions]] work on the eye at all; [[botanical accuracy]] underpins everything from [[Northern Renaissance]] symbolism to [[Pre-Raphaelite]] detail.

CORE TOPICS

• Skeletal and muscular anatomy sufficient for figure drawing

• The anatomy of the head, hands, and feet, the hardest and most revealing parts

• [[The physiology of vision]]: how the eye and brain construct colour, depth and motion

• [[Botany]]: [[plant structure]] and [[identification]], especially flowers with symbolic use in painting

• [[Comparative anatomy]] for animal painting

#### SUGGESTED READING & STUDY

• [[Andreas Vesalius]], [[De Humani Corporis Fabrica]] (illustrations)

• [[Leonardo da Vinci]]'s anatomical notebooks (selections)

• [[Bridgman's Complete Guide to Drawing from Life]]

• [[Margaret Livingstone]], [[Vision and Art - The Biology of Seeing]]

#### PRACTICAL EXERCISE

Draw the same hand from life six times in a single sitting, from six different angles, without reference to a photograph — until the underlying bone structure, not the surface skin, becomes what you are actually drawing.
