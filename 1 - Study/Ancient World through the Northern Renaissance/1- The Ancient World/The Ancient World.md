# Chapter I

# The Ancient World

### c. 3000 BCE – 400 CE

---
# Learning Objectives

By the end of this chapter, you should be able to answer:

### Civilisation

- Why did civilisation emerge around great rivers?
- Why were Egypt, Greece and Rome so different?
- How did geography shape culture?

---

### History

- Why did Egypt last for three thousand years?
- Why did Greece become hundreds of city states instead of one kingdom?
- Why did Rome conquer the Mediterranean?

---

### Religion

- Why were images sacred?
- Why did Greek gods look human?
- Why did Roman religion become political?
- How did Christianity begin within the Roman Empire?

---

### Philosophy

- Why did Greeks invent philosophy?
- What did Plato mean by beauty?
- Why did Aristotle influence painters for centuries?
- What is mimesis?

---

### Literature

- Why is Homer still important?
- How did myths shape painting?
- Why were epic stories painted for two thousand years?

---

### Architecture

- Why are Greek temples built the way they are?
- How were pyramids constructed?
- Why is Roman engineering still admired?

---

### Science

- What did ancient people understand about anatomy?
- How did astronomy influence religion?
- How accurate were Greek observations of nature?

---

### Society

- What was everyday life like?
- Who commissioned art?
- How did slavery influence civilisation?

---

### Art

- Why does Egyptian art never seem to change?
- Why did Greek sculpture become naturalistic?
- Why did Roman portraiture become so realistic?
- Why are Pompeii frescoes so important?

### Art History

### 1. [[The Story of Art — E.H. Gombrich]]

**Purpose:**  
Your main chronological guide.

Read:

- Chapters 1–5

Focus on:

- Egyptian conventions
- Greek naturalism
- Roman inheritance
- Early Christian art

Do not read passively. Every chapter should be paired with looking.

---

### 2. [[Art and Illusion — E.H. Gombrich]]

**Purpose:**  
Your philosophy of seeing.

Read selectively:

- Chapters on representation and imitation

Questions:

- Why do images become believable?
- What does "realism" actually mean?

---

### 3. [[The Classical Tradition — Gilbert Highet]]

**Purpose:**  
Understanding how Greece and Rome became the foundation of Western education.
