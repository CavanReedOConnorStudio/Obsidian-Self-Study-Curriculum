# Dutch Painting of the Golden Age.

This course is an adapted extract from the Open Universty course [[A226 Exploring art and visual culture]]


In this course, I will explore issues of meaing and interpretation with specific reference to Dutch 17th Century Painting. Works by artists [[Johannes Vermeer]], [[Pieter de Hooch]], [[Gabriel Metsu]], [[Jacob van Ruisdael]], and [[Jan Steen]] are among the best-known and most admired possessions in major collections. Unlike [[decorative]] schemes or [[mythological]] subjects, these paintings are comparitvaly small-scale appearing to require little prior knowledge of the part of the viewer. We have no difficulty understanding the scene represented and although the [[costumes]] and [[setting]] may be unfamiliar, we are offered a view on to a world that is recognisably continuous with our own. It is therefore surprising to learn there has been vigiorous debate on the meaning attributed to these paintings. Much of the debate has been centered on the purported [[realism]] of these paintings and the highly problematic assumption that they provide a comprehensive 'pictorial record' of life in [[the Dutch Republic]].  

## Learning Outcomes.

- explore recent debates around the interpretation of [[17th Century Painting]]
- consider the strengths and limitations of [[Iconography]] as an art-historical approach
- examine the notion of [[Realism]] as applied to works of art
- address the relevance of [[social]] and [[cultural]] context for interpreting works of art
- analyse works of art in terms of different ideas and approaches.
