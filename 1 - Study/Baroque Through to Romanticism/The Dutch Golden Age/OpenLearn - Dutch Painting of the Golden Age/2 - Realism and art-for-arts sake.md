# Introduction

> *It is evident that the fundamental tendency of this painting is above all **Realistic*** 
> - Wilhelm Bode, 1883

From the late [[19th Century]] to the mid [[20th Century]], it was widely accepted that Dutch painting of the golden age was distinguished from other national traditions by its realism. *[[Realism]]* seems to mean two different but related things.

1. Used to describe the subject matter of everyday life, such as domestic and tavern scenes. The term Realism here is a matter of *what* is being represented
2. Used to characterise a form of painting that purportedly offered a more truthful or exact imitation of visual appearances. The term Realism here is a matter of *how* an object or scene is represented. 
(Bialostocki, 1984)

Although these forms of realism frequently appear together, there is no necessary connection between them; it is possible to create a idealised image of a street scene, as it is possible to create a realistic image of royalty.
